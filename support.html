CelestEye support

Set your sky

Choose Use my sky to use your current location, or open Observing settings to enter coordinates or choose a city. Negative latitude is south; negative longitude is west.

Pointing looks wrong

Return to Now, update your current location, and move away from magnets, cars, and metal. Check the map against a known star. Use Align with a known bright star or the Moon, then check another object. Sensor alignment is approximate, and magnetic interference can cause drift. You can always stop tracking and drag the sky manually.

Camera AR

Select Camera AR on Sky and allow camera access when prompted. If access is off, open iPhone Settings, find CelestEye, and enable Camera. Return to Map and select Camera AR again. AR always shows the present time; event previews use the map. Camera images stay on your iPhone and are not recorded or uploaded.

Events and reminders

Open Find, then Events. Choose your observing location in Settings for local viewing windows. An event can highlight its approximate radiant or Sun/Moon location in the map. Meteor outlines locate the radiant; meteors can appear across the sky. Forecasts do not include clouds or terrain. To receive one local reminder, select Remind me and allow notifications. Check iPhone notification and Focus settings if alerts are quiet. After changing observing location, remove and reset the reminder.

Find an object

Open Find and search for a name or designation. Every official constellation remains searchable even below your horizon. Show in sky centers the map on supported objects; some moons, dwarf planets, and minor bodies contain reference information without a calculated sky position.

Offline use

Stars, constellations, packaged solar-system entries, sky calculations, manual locations, and time controls work offline. Additional JPL reference searches and StoreKit purchases require connectivity.
