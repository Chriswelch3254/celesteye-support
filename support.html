CelestEye support

Explore, choose, and find

Sky is your observing workspace. Tonight suggests a few useful targets, Events helps plan calendar opportunities, and Explore contains search, Saved, Recent, and reference content. Every observing feature is free; optional tips unlock nothing.

Set your sky

Open Observing settings to use current location, search an offline city, or choose a saved place. Coordinates and a civil time zone are available under Advanced coordinates. A planning place may differ from where your phone actually is. The context at the top of Sky identifies the active place and whether time is live or a preview.

Map, Point, and Camera AR

Tap the named mode at the top of Sky. Map lets you drag and pinch to explore. Point follows the phone without using the camera. Camera AR places sky annotations over the rear camera on a supported iPhone. Point and Camera AR use the present time; use Map for historical and future previews.

Choose a target and use Locate or Show in sky. Map can frame the target; live pointing retains the phone’s real direction and guides you toward it. Tap the target panel to read details. Objects in view provides a selectable text list. Sky tools opens time, layers, tracking details, and appearance controls.

Focus on the sky

Sky tools → Focus sky collapses optional controls and panels. Your mode, compact observing context, selected target, and important tracking warnings remain available. Show controls at the top restores the full controls; Night vision keeps its own Exit.

Pointing looks wrong or pauses

Check the place and time, move away from magnets, cars, and metal, and compare with a known bright star or planet. Use Align while keeping the real object on the reticle, then verify another object. One reference does not solve every orientation error. A marker near the reticle is not automatic recognition or a guarantee of accuracy.

If Guidance paused appears, use Retry or return to Map. Sensor freshness, compass quality, and manual alignment are separate. Tracking and conditions details explain the current reference and any restart. You can always return to Map and navigate manually.

Camera AR

Allow camera access when you choose Camera AR. If denied, enable Camera for CelestEye in iPhone Settings, or use Point/Map. After interruption, wait for orientation to settle and check alignment against a known object. If Camera not ready appears after startup, use Retry or switch to Map. The startup check begins after camera permission, so it does not rush your permission decision. Sky tools → Layers and appearance adjusts overlay intensity; it changes annotations, not camera exposure or alignment.

Camera images remain on your iPhone and are not recorded or uploaded. Camera AR does not identify stars from photographs or increase camera sensitivity. Calculated labels can appear even when an object is too faint for the camera to capture.

Tonight and useful time

Each target has a reason to look, an expected viewing method, a useful-time strip, and Locate. Strips use the same local observing session so you can compare intervals. The session runs noon to noon and can span midnight. Next-date and time-zone labels distinguish overnight and daylight-saving changes.

Open a target’s When section for its altitude curve, useful intervals, and optional sky path. Preview a time by using the chart or its menu; Now returns to the present. Windows use sampled geometry, darkness, and estimated Moon interference. They are not a cloud forecast. Highest altitude is not always the useful viewing time.

Events and reminders

Open the Events tab and choose your observing place for local viewing windows. Event details show times in sequence and can highlight a radiant or Sun/Moon location in Sky. Radiant outlines are locators; meteors cross a much wider sky. Terrain and weather are not included.

Choose Remind me to request one local notification. Check iPhone notification and Focus settings if alerts are quiet. A reminder keeps its original place and absolute time when you change places. Open its event to review and deliberately update or remove the plan. An ended notification opens explained reference context rather than pretending the event is still current.

Less on screen

Sky tools → Layers and appearance controls figure/name layers and label detail. Guide stars and estimated naked-eye visibility have separate controls. Focus sky hides secondary controls until you choose Show controls; it does not hide the active time/place or a tracking problem. An optional arrival haptic gives one gentle cue near the reticle.

Night vision and accessibility

Turn on Night vision in Observing settings → Night vision. It reduces bright colors and dims app-owned screens. It changes neither camera sensitivity nor tracking quality. Tap Exit at the top of Sky to leave Night vision. System keyboards, permission alerts, and external pages may remain bright.

Use iPhone text-size, contrast, reduced-transparency, and reduced-motion settings as needed. Objects in view and the chart’s interval menu provide alternatives to visual aiming and dragging. Scientific facts and full reference text remain available through the inspector’s disclosures.

Offline use and local data

The packaged catalog, sky calculations, offline cities/saved places, planning, and manual navigation work offline. Apple city searches, optional NASA/JPL reference searches, and support purchases need connectivity. Saved targets are not automatically marked Seen. Clear Recent or a deliberately captured tracking snapshot in settings; exporting a snapshot is optional.

Solar viewing

Never look directly at the Sun or through unfiltered binoculars or telescopes. CelestEye is not a solar-viewing filter. Use suitable solar observing equipment and guidance.
